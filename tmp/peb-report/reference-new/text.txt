ABSTRACT
To meet the requirements of today’s competitive world, engineering students must have
practical knowledge along with their theoretical knowledge. A postgraduate student of
structural engineering learns the analysis and design of members in the class, but the
exposure to a real project, where the loads, the architecture, the codes and the construction
practice come together, is possible only through an internship.
This report describes the four-week structural engineering training on “OM AKANT”, a
Ground + 13 storeyed residential-cum-commercial RCC building with two basement levels,
three blocks (A, B and C) and an estimated cost of Rs. 70 crore, located at Sargasan,
Gandhinagar. The building has shops on the Ground and First floor, 3 BHK, 4 BHK and
duplex apartments on the upper floors, a podium garden and a terrace amenity deck. The
structure is a dual system of RCC frames and shear walls (lift and staircase cores).
The first week deals with the preliminary information of the project, the type of structure, the
design loads (dead, live, wind and seismic) and the load cases. The second week covers the
analysis of the slab, beam, column, shear wall and staircase with shear force and bending
moment diagrams. The third week covers the proportioning and design of the members as per
IS 456:2000 and IS 13920:2016, and the fourth week covers the general notes for design and
detailing and the detailed drawings. A separate chapter explains the three-dimensional model
of the building prepared for analysis in ETABS and the deflection, drift and stability checks
that will be made.

CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION
1.1 General
Structural engineering is the branch of civil engineering that deals with the planning, analysis and
design of the load-carrying system of a building so that it remains safe, serviceable and economical
throughout its life. For a postgraduate student of Structural Engineering, theoretical knowledge of the
analysis and design of reinforced concrete members becomes meaningful only when it is applied to a
real building, with real architectural constraints, real loads and real construction practice. The
internship described in this report was carried out with this objective: to follow the complete
structural design process of a multi-storeyed mixed-use building, from the first collection of project
data up to the preparation of detailed structural drawings.
The building studied during the internship is “OM AKANT”, a Ground + 13 storeyed residential-
cum-commercial building with two basement levels, situated at Sargasan, Gandhinagar, Gujarat. The
project is being developed by Om Buildcon. The building is a reinforced cement concrete (RCC)
framed structure supported by shear walls / lift cores and is divided into three blocks, namely Block
A, Block B and Block C. The lower floors (Ground and First floor) are planned as shops
(commercial use), whereas the upper floors accommodate 3 BHK, 4 BHK and duplex apartments
(residential use) together with a landscaped podium and a terrace-level amenity deck.
This report presents the four weeks of structural training in a chapter-wise manner. Week 1 covers
preliminary information of the project, types of structure, design load calculation and load case
details. Week 2 covers analysis of structural members, shear force and bending moment of various
structural elements. Week 3 covers proportioning and dimensions of structural members and design
of the members. Week 4 covers the general notes for design and detailing and the detailed drawings
of all structural members. A separate chapter explains the three-dimensional model that is to be
prepared and run in ETABS for the complete building.
1.2 About the Project
OM AKANT is positioned as a premium residential address with ground-level retail. The
development consists of 3 BHK and 4 BHK apartments and a small number of duplex units, a
basement parking facility on two levels, shops on the Ground and First floors and a rich set of
community amenities such as a party lawn, splash pool, amphitheatre, skating rink, multipurpose
court, indoor games room, digital library and pergola sit-outs. The architectural theme of the building
is the undulating, wave-like balcony edge which can clearly be seen on the elevation of the building
(Figure 2.1 to Figure 2.3) and which also strongly influences the structural layout, because the
balconies are formed by curved cantilever slabs and balcony beams.

1.3 Location and Site Details
The project is located at Sargasan in Gandhinagar, on the S.G. Highway corridor, which is one of the
fastest-growing residential and commercial belts between Ahmedabad and Gandhinagar. The plot is a
Final Plot (FP-11) of Town Planning Scheme No. 26 and is surrounded by roads on two sides: a 36 m
wide road on the east / north-east and an 18 m wide road on the south side, as shown on the ground
floor plan of the brochure. The shops are therefore oriented towards the roads, while the residential
entrance, drop-off zone and landscaped podium are arranged along the inner side of the plot.
Gandhinagar lies in Seismic Zone III as per IS 1893 (Part 1): 2016, and the basic wind speed for the
area is 39 m/s as per IS 875 (Part 3): 2015. The soil conditions in the region are generally of the
medium soil type. These three data decide the lateral load effects on the building and are used in
Chapter 3 and Chapter 7.

1.4 Type of Structure
The building is a multi-storeyed RCC framed structure. The vertical load path is: slab →
secondary / main beams →columns (and shear walls at the lift and staircase cores) →foundation.
Lateral loads due to earthquake and wind are resisted by the combined action of the moment-resisting
frames and the RCC core walls around the lifts and staircases, so the building behaves as a dual
(frame + shear wall) system. The floor system consists of cast-in-situ two-way slabs of 150 mm and
175 mm thickness (as written on the structural layout drawings) supported on beams, the stair cases
are of the waist-slab type, and the curved balconies are cantilever slabs supported on balcony beams
(marked BK1 / SBK1 on the drawings).
Because the Ground and First floors are shops and the upper floors are apartments, the column grid of
the lower floors has to give large clear spans for the shops and for the two-level basement parking,
whereas on the upper floors the columns are fitted into the walls of the apartments. The structural
consultant achieves this by keeping the columns on the same grid lines through all floors as far as
possible, and by using deeper beams where the grid changes. The staircases and lift shafts are
designed as RCC cores which also increase the lateral stiffness of the building.

1.5 Concept of Residential cum Commercial Planning
Residential-cum-commercial (mixed-use) buildings combine the revenue of retail space with the
demand for apartments. In OM AKANT the following planning concept has been followed:
•
Basements (two levels): car and two-wheeler parking with ramp, driveways and lift /
staircase lobbies of each block reaching the basement.
•
Ground floor: shops (Shop 01 to Shop 22, including sub-numbered shops) facing the roads,
foyers of Block A, B and C, a security cabin, entrance gate, drop-off zone, and the landscaped
podium with walkway, splash pool, party lawn, amphitheatre, skating rink, human-size ludo
and multipurpose court.
•
First floor: shops (Shop 101 to Shop 114) with 7'2" wide passage, lifts and staircases
continuing to the upper floors.
•
Second floor: first residential floor with units 201, 202 and 203 and large open terraces over
the shop podium.
•
Third to Thirteenth floor: typical apartment floors – 4 BHK units in Block A and 3 BHK
units in Block B and Block C; twelfth floor of Block C has duplex units (1201, 1202 and
1203).
•
Terrace: common amenity deck with indoor game area, pergola sitting, yoga deck, gazebo,
senior citizen sitting area, open air amphitheatre, central sitting plaza, Zula court, barbecue
area and digital library.
Separate entry foyers and lifts are provided for the residents of each block, and the commercial users
enter directly from the road, so that the movement of shop customers and residents does not mix.

3.3.4 Earthquake load (IS 1893 Part 1: 2016)
The design horizontal seismic coefficient is Ah = (Z / 2) × (I / R) × (Sa / g). For OM AKANT: zone
factor Z = 0.16 (Zone III), importance factor I = 1.2 (large residential-cum-commercial building with
more than 200 occupants), response reduction factor R = 5 (dual system with ductile shear walls and
special moment-resisting frames, to be confirmed by the consultant) and medium soil (Type II).
The approximate fundamental natural period of an RC building with brick infill walls is Ta = 0.09 h /
√d. Taking h = 44.1 m and an assumed plan dimension d = 20 m, Ta = 0.89 s. For medium soil, Sa/g
= 1.36 / T = 1.53, hence Ah = (0.16 / 2) × (1.2 / 5) × 1.53 = 0.0294. The design base shear is VB =
Ah × W, where W is the seismic weight of the building (DL + percentage of LL). The vertical
distribution is made by Qi = VB × Wi hi² / Σ Wj hj². The final period will be taken from the modal
analysis in ETABS.

The analysis of the structure was understood in two levels. At the first level, each member is analysed
manually using the code coefficients and the basic equations of statics, so that the engineer has a
feeling for the expected size of the forces. At the second level, the complete three-dimensional model
is analysed in ETABS (Chapter 7) and the software results are compared with the manual values. The
load path assumed for the manual analysis is slab →beam →column →footing.

discrepancy the structural consultant shall be informed.
2. All the work shall be carried out as per IS 456:2000, IS 13920:2016, IS 875, IS 1893 and SP

CHAPTER 7: MODELLING AND ANALYSIS IN ETABS
7.1 About ETABS
ETABS (Extended Three-dimensional Analysis of Building Systems), developed by Computers and
Structures Inc. (CSI), is a special-purpose software for the analysis and design of multi-storeyed
buildings. It works on the finite element method: columns and beams are modelled as frame (line)
elements and slabs, shear walls and ramps are modelled as shell (area) elements. The software has the
facility of automatic generation of the wind and seismic loads as per Indian codes (IS 875 Part 3 and
IS 1893 Part 1), of the load combinations as per IS 456, of the response spectrum and modal analysis,
and of the concrete frame design as per IS 456 and IS 13920. The output includes member forces,
storey displacements, storey drifts, base reactions, modal periods and the reinforcement required in
beams, columns and walls.
The complete structure of OM AKANT (Block A, Block B and Block C, with two basements,
Ground to 13th floor and terrace) will be analysed in ETABS by the intern as a single three-
dimensional model. The model is prepared and is to be run in ETABS; the screenshots of the
model and the results are to be inserted at the places that are kept blank in this chapter.
7.2 Modelling Steps
1. Grid and storey data: the grid lines are created from the architectural centre-line drawing;
the storey heights are defined from the basement to the terrace (assumed 3.15 m typical floor-
to-floor height; basement and ground floor as per section).
2. Material properties: concrete M30 and M40 (Ec = 5000 √fck), steel Fe 500D (fy = 500
N/mm²), unit weight of concrete 25 kN/m³, Poisson’s ratio 0.2.
3. Section properties: columns, beams, slabs (membrane / shell thin) and walls are defined with
the sizes of Chapter 5; the cracked section modifiers are applied as per IS 1893 / IS 456
(beams 0.35 Ig, columns 0.7 Ig, slab 0.25 Ig, walls 0.7 Ig for the seismic analysis).
4. Drawing the elements: the columns, beams, slabs, shear walls, lift cores, staircase landings
and the cantilever balcony slabs are drawn on each storey and replicated for similar floors.
5. Supports: the base of the columns and walls is considered fixed at the foundation level (or
springs for the raft if the soil stiffness is available); the basement retaining walls are modelled
as walls with lateral spring supports or as fixed at the basement slab level.
6. Diaphragms: a rigid diaphragm is assigned to every floor slab; semi-rigid diaphragm is used
at the podium level where slab openings are large.
7. Load patterns: DL (self weight multiplier = 1), SDL (floor finish and wall loads as per
Chapter 3), LL, EQX, EQY (IS 1893:2016), WX and WY (IS 875 Part 3: 2015).
8. Mass source: 100% DL + 25% LL (50% LL for the shops); no live load on the roof.

9. Load combinations: the 12 combinations of Table 3.9 are defined (the envelope of all
combinations is also created).
10. Analysis options: modal analysis (at least 12 modes; mass participation of 90% in each
direction), response spectrum analysis, P-Δ effect (considered as per IS 456 for slender
members), and check of the model with “Analyze – Run”.
11. Design: concrete frame design as per IS 456 + IS 13920 and shear wall design with pier
labels; the results are compared with the manual design of Chapter 5.

CHAPTER 8: CONCLUSION
The four-week internship gave a complete view of the structural design of a real multi-
storeyed building, OM AKANT, a Ground + 13 storeyed residential-cum-commercial RCC
building with two basements at Sargasan, Gandhinagar. In the first week the architectural
information, the type of structure and the design loads were studied. In the second week the
slab, beam, column, shear wall and staircase were analysed and the shear force and bending
moment diagrams were drawn. In the third week the members were proportioned and
designed as per IS 456:2000 and IS 13920:2016, and in the fourth week the general notes, the
detailing rules and the structural drawings were studied.
The main learning outcomes of the internship are:
•
Reading and understanding of structural layout drawings (slab-beam grids, beam
schedules, column schedules).
•
Calculation of dead, live, wind and seismic loads and preparation of the load
combinations as per the Indian standards.
•
Understanding of the load path and of the importance of the lift cores / shear walls in
a dual system.
•
Manual analysis and design of the slab, beam and column, and understanding of the
ductile detailing requirements of IS 13920.
•
Understanding of the ETABS modelling procedure and of the serviceability checks of
deflection and storey drift.
•
Understanding of the coordination between the architect, the structural consultant, the
MEP consultant and the site team.
The author is grateful for the opportunity to work on such a project, and the knowledge
gained will be used in the dissertation work and in the future career in the field of structural
engineering.
